Book Reflection

The Richest Man In Babylon

Author: George S. Clason

Delving into the principles of wealth creation, saving, and financial responsibility and how they could align with my long-term professional and personal goals.

👉 "Richest Man in Babylon"

Chapter 3 Reflection: "7 Cures for a Lean Purse"

Summary

In this chapter, the Babylonian king learns from a council member that most citizens are poor despite the city's recent infrastructural developments. To make Babylon the wealthiest place, he decides that its citizens must be wealthy as well. Hence, why the king summons Arkad, the richest man in Babylon, to educate 100 teachers on the principles of wealth creation.

Personal Take Away From This Chapter

Wealth is about making your money work persistently through sound investments and allowing accumulated interest to do the heavy lifting over time. It is not just about stockpiling money in a stagnant savings account. "Seven Cures for a Lean Purse" highlights that increasing one's income begins with improving themself by developing focus, determination, and genuine skill in their line of work.

In order to integrate this into my life, I must currently use my education at UTech as my main route to accumulating wealth. I have to protect my time and focus from distractions, similar to how Arkad suggests protecting your riches from loss by speaking with experienced mentors and carefully researching opportunities before charging in blindly. The most important lesson I learned is that career achievement and financial independence are developed gradually through simple, deliberate budgets and ongoing personal development until you are genuinely exceptional at what you do.

Chapter 5 Reflection: "5 Laws of Gold"

Summary

In Chapter 5, Nomasier, the son of Arkad, is presented with a clay tablet of wisdom and a bag of gold to demonstrate his value. Nomasier humbles himself, learns the five rules of gold, and uses them to achieve enormous success after he carelessly loses his first wealth. These basic rules—saving ten percent of earnings, investing in viable companies, protecting capital with cautious advice, avoiding unfamiliar ventures, and avoiding unachievable, scheme-driven returns—are outlined in the chapter.

Personal Take Away From This Chapter

Nomasier's initial arrogance resonates with me as a student. Without seeking guidance from mentors or establishing a solid foundation, it is simple for someone to assume that they know how to handle money or deal with difficult situations. The most important lesson I learned from this chapter is that while making mistakes is a necessary part of learning, in order to have true financial intelligence, it is required to have the humility to learn the rules, pay attention to educated advisors, and acknowledge that "there is no chain of disaster that will not come to an end" as long as you are dedicated to studying and putting what you have learnt into practice.

My Biggest Take Away

Success and wealth are based on discipline, humility, and continuous growth rather than luck, shortcuts, or straight cash. True financial security involves a willingness to learn from knowledgeable individuals, living within your means, and building your skills over time, whether you follow the seven cures to grow a lean purse or the five laws of gold to protect and multiply your earnings.
