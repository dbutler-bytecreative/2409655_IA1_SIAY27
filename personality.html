This instantly improves my software design and web development capabilities. 2. Strategic Gaming & Systems Thinking

Gaming is more than just a hobby, in reality, it serves as a training ground for resource management, detecting patterns, and strategic adaptation in the face of unforeseen challenges; whether it be in programming, academic or daily life. Complex games are similar to software development. When an application crashes or a logic loop breaks, instinctively, I will be able to approach troubleshooting like surpassing a level or challenge in a game: examining variables, testing theories, and repeating until I am successful.

I am an introverted developer that does best when I am in solitary, focused environments. I approach breaking down tricky code structures methodically and calmly rather than rushing. Inspirational Figure

Erwin Smith

Background

Erwin Smith is a fictional character from the infamous anime: "Attack On Titan". He is the 13th commander of the Survey Corps, succeeded Keith Shadis and is recognized for his innovative tactics, notably the Long-Distance Enemy Scouting Formation, which minimized casualties in operations. Despite his concern for his men, Erwin is willing to be ruthless to uncover the truth about the world, even sacrificing soldiers and risking civilian lives.

"If You Begin To Regret, You'll Dull Your Future Decisions And Let Others Make Your Choices For You."

Why Does He Inspire Me?

Erwin Smith is one of my primary inspirations because of his remarkable ability to make tough, calculated judgements under extreme pressure without losing sight of the end goal. Unexpected issues and strict deadlines often feel like overwhelming obstacles in software development and in academic work as a whole. I feel resilient whenever I think of Erwin's philosophy, which emphasises that we must overcome uncertainty and grow from our mistakes rather than giving in to regret.

Critical Reflection: LMMs as a Tool, Not a Crutch

It's a Tool, Don't Treat it Like Your Crutch

Treating AI as a crutch, in my opinion, contributes to eradicating the thought process of learning; in a lot of cases, having an algorithm develop code or draft assignments from scratch—which eventually results in the loss of critical thinking abilities. The statement itself draws attention to a crucial contrast in modern education in comparison to the past, before Artificial Intelligence was popularised. Technology should enhance human cognition rather than take its place. However, utilising AI as a tool involves maintaining the ability to actively engage with the learning process. I can successfully use LLMs throughout the course of my studies to look for educational resources, explain difficult programming topics, or act as an interactive system for organising ideas at the early stages of research and brainstorming. I would be able to quicken my academic progress without sacrificing my integrity or problem-solving skills by using AI to explain difficult logical errors or summarise hefty source material while I actually write the real code and papers myself.
